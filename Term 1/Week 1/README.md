## 2. Hackathon prototype -> [`hackathon/`](hackathon/)

> Your tool and your SDG for this hackathon are announced at the **start of Friday's class**.

**Project title:**
Scrolly

**My pair partner:**
Minhyeok Sung

**Tool we had to use:**
Lovable AI

**SDG we had to address:**

SDG 4 – Quality Education,
specifically Target 4.1: Quality primary and secondary education and effective learning outcomes, and Target 4.5: Equal access to education and reducing inequalities in education.
Scrolly supports Target 4.1 by helping students actively learn and check their understanding of educational material rather than simply reading it. The interactive quizzes provide immediate feedback, helping learners identify what they understand and what they need to review.
It also contributes to Target 4.5 by making dense educational content more accessible and approachable for students with different learning preferences and reading abilities, including learners who may struggle with traditional long-form text.

**What problem does it solve, and for whom?**

Scrolly helps students who struggle with long and difficult study materials. It turns textbooks, notes, and articles into short, interactive cards with concepts, definitions, examples, quizzes, instant feedback, and summaries. It also supports 5 languages, making learning more accessible.

**What did you build?**

We built Scrolly, an AI-powered learning app that transforms educational text into a sequence of interactive cards. Users can paste their study material and learn through concepts, definitions, examples, “why it matters” explanations, quick-check questions, instant feedback, and a final summary. Scrolly also supports 5 languages, allowing users to translate their study material and learn in their preferred language.

**Link to the live thing (if any):**
https://studyscrolly.lovable.app

**How do I run it?**

Open the Scrolly website in a browser. 
Paste your study material into the input box and choose your preferred language from the 5 available languages. 
Click Generate, and Scrolly uses AI to turn the material into short, interactive learning cards.
Scroll down through the cards to move from one concept to the next, with definitions, examples, and “Why it matters” sections along the way. You’ll also get quick quizzes with instant feedback to test your understanding, followed by a final summary of what you learned.

**Who did what?**

Pavitra: Came up with the main idea of making long and difficult study materials easier and more engaging to learn. I helped develop the overall concept of Scrolly, contributed to the design and learning features, and worked on building, testing, and improving the prototype. I also contributed to the README, ethical reflection, and presentation.

Minhyeok: Came up with the idea of turning the study material into flashcard-style content that users can scroll through, creating the main interaction that shaped Scrolly’s user experience. They contributed to developing and designing the prototype, testing and improving its features, and worked with me on the README, ethical reflection, and presentation.

**Ethical reflection - what are the risks of your tool? Who could it harm?**

From our point of view, Scrolly is designed to make studying easier and more engaging, but it may not be accessible to every student. Students without internet access, a suitable device, or one of our five supported languages may have difficulty using it. Since AI creates the learning cards, it could sometimes leave out or simplify important information. There is also a possibility that students could use Scrolly just to scroll through the cards and guess the quiz answers without actually learning. This could give them a false sense of understanding, so we added quizzes and instant feedback to encourage students to actively engage with the material.
